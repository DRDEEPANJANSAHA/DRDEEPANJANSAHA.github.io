Zero Trust That Measures Itself: The AI Plane Extension

Zero trust on the network plane is mature. The open question is the AI plane: an agentic AI stack is a new workload class — standing tool grants, attacker-writable memory, and outputs that trigger actions. SAHA31 extends the same deny-by-default, verify-continuously doctrine to that plane, and it works because zero trust, done properly, is a measured discipline: you run 40+ threat-actor emulation paths across your segmentation and measure how far each one advances.

01The Blueprint (Verified: 94% Lateral-Movement Risk Reduction)

  • Microsegmentation with cost, not just blocks. Lateral movement isn't prevented — it's made expensive and observable. Measured across 100,000+ protected enterprise seats.
  • Least-privilege, continuous. Identity, device posture, and scope are re-verified per request; standing privileges are the exception that gets hunted, not the default.
  • The AI extension (L3/L5). Signed tool manifests, capability-union grants, deterministic egress allowlists, and a tested kill-switch: an agent that is breached can move, but it cannot act.
  • The metric that matters. Lateral-movement risk reduction, trended — the same residual-risk-with-a-trend-line deliverable the SAHA31 gate produces for AI systems.

02What You Get as a Deliverable

Not a policy document. A measured risk-reduction number with a trend line, the threat-actor paths that prove it, and the agent-side controls (manifests, gating, kill-switch) wired into your tool layer — with the gate enforcing both planes in CI.
