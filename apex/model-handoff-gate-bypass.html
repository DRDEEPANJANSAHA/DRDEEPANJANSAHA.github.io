Model-Handoff Gate Bypass in Routed Systems

Model & Weights · L4 · fine-tunes + quants · Model & Weights

Routed systems hand work between models (a big model plans, a small one executes, a router decides), and every handoff is a trust seam: the router can be steered into the weaker model, the executor can receive a plan written by a compromised planner. The system is only as safe as its weakest handoff.

01Control Architecture

How it is defended

Handoff gating: each seam carries its own check (input provenance, plan-integrity scan, model-capability tier match), and router steering is itself an attack row — 'get the task onto the weak model' is in the catalog with an owner layer. The system's residual risk is the product across seams, which is why seams belong in the six-layer model at all.

03FAQ

How do model handoffs become an attack surface?

Every seam is a trust boundary: a router steered to the weak model, a compromised planner's plan, an executor that trusts its input. Gate each seam (provenance, integrity, tier match), count router steering as an attack row, and compute residual risk across seams as a product.
